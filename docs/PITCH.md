# AaykarDesk — Sales Pitch for CAs & CA Firms

> **From notice to response — without switching tabs.**

*A field guide for pitching AaykarDesk to Chartered Accountants and CA firms. Use it as
talking points in a meeting, a leave-behind after a demo, or the script for a cold walk-in.*

---

## 1. The one-line pitch

**AaykarDesk turns a 4–6 hour scrutiny-notice scramble into a 30-minute, single-screen workflow — without replacing a single tool your firm already trusts.**

If you only have 30 seconds with a partner, say exactly that, then offer the demo.

---

## 2. The pain we're selling against

Walk into any practice and you'll hear the same story. When an income tax **scrutiny
notice** lands (143(2), 142(1), reassessment, penalty), the clerk runs a five-tool relay:

| Step | Tool | What goes wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Log the notice & deadline | Jamku / ATOM Pro | Deadline tracking lives in someone's head |
| Read & decode the notice | The PDF itself | Section, AY, AO, regime, annexure docs — all manual |
| Pull the books | TallyPrime | Re-keying numbers, tab-switching |
| Research the law | Taxmann.AI | 1961 ↔ 2025 Act cross-referencing is now a chore |
| Chase the client | Phone / WhatsApp | Blurry photos, wrong files, endless follow-ups |
| Draft & file | Word + IT Portal | Copy-paste, formatting, re-uploading |

**Five-plus tools. Five-plus context switches. 4–6 hours of pure admin per notice.**
Multiply that across a notice season and it's the single biggest drain on a firm's
billable capacity.

> **The opening question to ask a partner:**
> *"When a 143(2) lands on your desk, how many tabs does your clerk have open — and how
> many hours before the response is actually drafted?"*
> Let them answer. The number is always painful. That's your wedge.

---

## 3. The positioning that wins the room

The objection you'll hear first is *"We already pay for Jamku / Tally / Taxmann — why
another tool?"* The answer is the heart of the pitch:

> **AaykarDesk is not a replacement. It's the orchestration layer.**

| Tool | Stays the system of record for |
| --- | --- |
| **Jamku / ATOM Pro** | Notice tracking & DSC management |
| **TallyPrime** | Books of accounts & reconciliation |
| **Taxmann.AI** | Legal research & case law |
| **ClearTax / Saral** | Return filing |

AaykarDesk connects these into **one case workflow** and removes the manual glue between
them. Nobody has to rip and replace. Nobody has to retrain on a new system of record.
We sit *on top* of the stack they already love — and that framing disarms the
"yet-another-subscription" reflex.

## 4. The demo — 5 beats, ~6 minutes

This is the part that sells itself. The product runs **end-to-end with no backend and no
setup**, so you can demo it on a laptop, on airport Wi-Fi, or with the Wi-Fi off entirely.

> *"Let me show you how AaykarDesk fits into your existing workflow."*

1. **The problem (15s).** "Today your clerk gets a 143(2). They log it in Jamku, check
   Tally, search Taxmann, WhatsApp the client, draft in Word. Five tools, five switches,
   deadline in their head."
2. **The upload (30s).** Drop the notice PDF. In ~3 seconds AaykarDesk extracts
   *everything* — section, assessment year, deadline, AO, ward, and **every document the
   annexure demands**. It even flags that this is a **Faceless** assessment.
3. **The client handoff (60s).** "See these 8 documents? Normally your clerk calls or
   WhatsApps and gets back 4 blurry photos and 2 wrong files. Instead — **one link**." The
   client opens a clean mobile checklist, uploads each file, and the CA is notified when
   it's complete. No app install, no login for the client.
4. **The intelligence (60s).** Open the **Research** tab — Section 143(2) is already mapped
   to its **2025 Act equivalent**, the relevant Rules are listed, and there's a pre-built
   **Taxmann.AI deep link** for the exact issues raised. "Your clerk would have spent 45
   minutes finding this. It's already in the case file." Then the **Reconciliation** tab:
   "As per Notice / 26AS" beside "As per Client Records," with mismatches highlighted.
5. **The ask (30s).** "Everything your clerk does today still happens. Jamku still tracks.
   Tally still holds the books. Taxmann is still your research bible. AaykarDesk just
   deletes the 4–6 hours of admin in between."

**Tip:** the demo ships with six pre-seeded cases (faceless, jurisdictional, and a
Tally-imported reconciliation), so even without uploading you have a populated dashboard
to walk through if the room is short on time.

## 6. The ROI math (make it concrete)

Help the partner do the arithmetic out loud:

- **Time saved:** ~4 hours of admin per notice → reclaimed for billable work or more cases.
- **A firm handling 20 scrutiny notices a season** saves **~80 hours** — two full work
  weeks of a senior clerk's time.
- **Fewer missed deadlines:** firm-wide deadline tracking instead of "it was in my head."
- **Faster client turnaround:** one link vs. days of WhatsApp chasing → documents in hours.
- **Junior leverage:** a first-year article can run a notice that used to need a senior,
  because the extraction, checklist, and research are done for them.

> The clincher: *"If AaykarDesk saves one clerk two weeks a season, what's that worth to
> you — and how does that compare to the subscription?"*

## 7. Objection handling

| If they say… | You say… |
| --- | --- |
| *"We already have Jamku/Tally/Taxmann."* | "Good — keep them. We don't replace any of them. We connect them and kill the admin in between." |
| *"Is my client data safe?"* | "Every firm's data is fully isolated — strict row-level security so a firm only ever sees its own cases. Notice PDFs and client uploads live in private storage, reachable only through short-lived signed links." |
| *"Will the AI get the notice wrong?"* | "Extraction is a *first draft* the CA confirms — every field carries a confidence score, and nothing is filed without your review. It removes typing, not judgment." |
| *"My clients aren't tech-savvy."* | "That's the point of the magic link — no app, no login. They tap a link, see a checklist, upload photos from their phone. Simpler than WhatsApp." |
| *"Setup sounds painful."* | "Sign in with Google and you're in. We can run the whole thing in demo mode on your laptop right now — no IT project." |
| *"What about the new 2025 Act?"* | "Built in. Every section is mapped both ways, 1961 ↔ 2025, automatically." |

## 8. Who to target first

- **Sweet spot:** 3–15 professional firms that handle a steady flow of scrutiny/assessment
  work — enough volume to feel the pain, small enough to decide fast.
- **The champion:** the **partner who signs the responses** (feels the deadline risk) or the
  **senior who manages the clerks** (feels the admin drain).
- **The trap to avoid:** pitching the clerk first — they may see automation as a threat.
  Pitch the partner the *capacity* story; position the tool as the clerk's co-pilot.

---

## 9. Discovery questions for the meeting

1. How many scrutiny / assessment notices do you handle in a season?
2. Who reads the notice and decides what documents to collect — and how long does it take?
3. How do you collect documents from clients today? How often are they wrong or late?
4. How do you track deadlines across the firm right now?
5. How much of the 1961 ↔ 2025 cross-referencing are you doing by hand?
6. If you got 4 hours back per notice, what would the firm do with it?

> *"Give me one real notice and one of your clerks for fifteen minutes. We'll run it through

> do twenty more times this season."*

Offer a **no-risk pilot**: a handful of live cases, demo mode or a sandboxed firm account,
and a check-in after the first week. Let the time saved make the argument for you.
